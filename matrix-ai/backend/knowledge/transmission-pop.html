Definition of a POP

A POP (Point of Presence) is a physical site where Matrix Telecom installs its network equipment to connect customers to the Internet. It is the central service point for a geographic area. Each POP is a mini-datacenter that hosts routers, switches, OLTs, fiber patch panels, batteries, and power supplies.

Role of the POP in the Network

The POP is the convergence point between Matrix's Internet backbone and end customers. It performs several critical functions:

  • Aggregation: Collects traffic from all customers in the area
  • Distribution: Distributes connectivity to customers via fiber, microwave, or copper
  • Routing: Routes traffic to Matrix's Internet backbone
  • Redundancy: Ensures service continuity in case of failure
  • Power: Provides stabilized and backup power to equipment

Redundancy and High Availability

Network Redundancy

  • Dual backbone link: Each POP connects to the main POP via two distinct paths (fiber + microwave)
  • Backup router: VRRP (Virtual Router Redundancy Protocol) with a second standby router
  • LACP: Link aggregation between router and distribution switch to avoid single point of failure
  • Redundant VLANs: Spanning Tree Protocol (RSTP/MSTP) to prevent loops and ensure resilience

Power Redundancy

  • Dual power: Each device is plugged into two separate PDUs (A and B)
  • UPS: Filters voltage variations and provides 15-30 minutes of autonomy
  • Batteries: External batteries (48V DC) extend autonomy to 2-4 hours
  • Generator: For critical POPs, a generator takes over after 30 seconds
  • Surge protectors: Protection against surges on every incoming line (mains and network cables)

Cooling Redundancy

  • Two independent air conditioners (N+1)
  • Each AC can cool the POP alone if the other fails
  • Temperature probe with SMS alert if T° > 30°C or < 15°C

Practical Case: Visiting a Matrix POP

Guided Tour of the Matrix Mvan POP in Yaoundé

Here is what you will see when visiting the Matrix Mvan POP:

  1. Entrance: Reinforced door with electronic lock + keypad. Only authorized personnel have access.
  2. Fire extinguisher: CO2 extinguisher (suitable for electrical equipment) next to the door.
  3. 42U Rack: Black closed rack with glass door. Ambient temperature: 22°C.
  4. Top of rack (U1-U4): ODF panel with 48 SC/APC fibers. Each fiber is labeled with its destination.
  5. U5-U8: Huawei MA5800-X15 OLT. Pigtail cables run from the ODF to the OLT.
  6. U9-U12: MikroTik CRS328 distribution switch. Green SFP+ fiber connections to the router.
  7. U13-U16: MikroTik CCR1072 core router. 10G fiber link to the central POP (Bastos).
  8. U17-U18: Copper patch bay with Cat6a patch cables to the router and switch.
  9. U19-U20: APC PDU with consumption monitoring. Two independent PDUs (A and B).
  10. U21-U22: APC Smart-UPS 3000VA. Green LEDs: everything is normal.
  11. U23-U24: External batteries 48V 100Ah.
  12. Bottom of rack: Free space for expansion.
  13. Outside the rack: 12000 BTU wall-mounted AC, ceiling smoke detector, IP camera.
  14. On the roof: Parabolic antennas (microwave link to Bastos POP) and 4G backup antenna.

POP Intervention Procedure

  1. Prerequisites: Report arrival to the NOC. Wear PPE (insulated shoes, anti-static gloves).
  2. Verification: Check temperature, humidity, LED status.
  3. Inspection: Visually check each device. Identify any alarms.
  4. Intervention: If replacement or maintenance, follow the specific procedure.
  5. Documentation: Record the intervention in the POP logbook.
  6. Closure: Verify everything is functional. Close the door. Report to NOC.

Tools Used for POP Maintenance

Digital Multimeter (DC Power Meter)
Usage:
  • Measure battery voltage (48V DC nominal, between 42V and 54V)
  • Check mains input voltage (220-240V AC)
  • Measure current draw of each device (ammeter mode)
  • Verify ground continuity
# Typical measurements in a Matrix POP
Battery voltage: 53.2V DC (good, battery charged)
Mains voltage: 231V AC (within norms)
Router consumption: 0.45A at 48V = 21.6W (correct)
OLT consumption: 3.8A at 48V = 182.4W (correct)
Thermometer / Hygrometer with Probe
Usage:
  • Measure temperature at several locations in the POP (top, middle, bottom of rack)
  • Check ambient humidity
  • Ideal: 20-24°C, 40-55% RH
  • Alert threshold: > 30°C or < 15°C, RH > 70%
  • Use an infrared thermometer for hotspots
# Measurement points in a POP
Top of rack (air intake): 21.5°C, 48% RH
Middle of rack (OLT): 23.8°C
Bottom of rack (UPS): 22.1°C
AC output: 14.5°C
Outside temperature: 32°C
Thermal Camera
Usage:
  • Detect abnormal hot spots on equipment
  • Identify a failing fan
  • Locate overheating equipment
  • Models: Flir E8, Seek Thermal, or smartphone accessory
Electrical Outlet Tester
Usage:
  • Verify electrical outlet wiring (phase, neutral, ground)
  • Detect phase-neutral reversal or missing ground
  • Models: Fluke T90, Klein Tools RT105

POP Monitoring

POPs are monitored 24/7 by Matrix's NOC (Network Operations Center) via:

  • SNMP: Each device sends its indicators (CPU, memory, traffic, temperature)
  • Nagios / Zabbix: Alerts in case of failure or threshold exceeded
  • Syslog: Centralized logging of all devices
  • Temperature: Probe with SMS alert if temperature exceeds 30°C
  • IP camera: Video surveillance of the POP
# Example SNMP check of a POP
snmpwalk -v2c -c public 10.0.0.1 1.3.6.1.2.1.1.5.0  # POP name
snmpwalk -v2c -c public 10.0.0.1 1.3.6.1.2.1.1.3.0  # Uptime
snmpwalk -v2c -c public 10.0.0.1 .1.3.6.1.4.1.14988.1.1.3.8.0  # MikroTik CPU temperature

Tip for the Intern

Tip:
  • ASK TO VISIT A POP! It is the best way to learn. Observe everything, take notes and photos (with permission).
  • Before entering a POP, make sure you have the necessary authorizations and are accompanied by a senior technician.
  • Never touch any equipment without explicit instruction. Mishandling can cut off access for hundreds of customers.
  • Learn to recognize equipment LEDs (green = OK, orange = warning, red = fault). Each device has its own code.
  • In a POP, noise is normal (fans). If the noise changes pitch, investigate (probable fan failure).
  • Always check the last battery maintenance date. Lead batteries last 3-5 years.
  • Keep a stock of consumables in each POP: RJ45 cables of various lengths, labels, velcro, connectors.
  • Note the GPS coordinates of the POP. Useful for emergency interventions.
