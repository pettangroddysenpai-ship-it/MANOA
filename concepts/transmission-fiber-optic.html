Fiber Optic — FTTH

← Back to Index

Definition

Fiber optic is a transmission medium that uses light (laser or LED) to carry data at very high speeds over long distances. At Matrix Telecom, fiber optic is the flagship technology for FTTH (Fiber To The Home) connections deployed in the neighborhoods of Yaoundé. It offers symmetric speeds up to 1 Gb/s with unmatched reliability.

Components of an FTTH Network

OLT (Optical Line Terminal)

Active equipment located at Matrix's POP (Point of Presence). It is the central point of the GPON (Gigabit Passive Optical Network). It manages communication with up to 64 or 128 ONU/ONT. The OLT emits a laser signal at 1490 nm for downstream data and receives at 1310 nm for upstream data.

ONU / ONT (Optical Network Unit / Terminal)

Equipment installed at the customer premises. It converts the optical signal into an electrical signal (Ethernet). The ONT is the fiber network termination point at the subscriber's location. Common models at Matrix include Huawei HG8245, Nokia G-240W-A, and Ubiquiti UFiber ONTs.

GPON (Gigabit Passive Optical Network)

Point-to-multipoint technology where a single fiber serves multiple subscribers through passive optical splitters. Typical distribution:

  • Splitter 1:4 at the POP (1st stage)
  • Splitter 1:8 or 1:16 at the distribution point (2nd stage)
  • Total: 1:32 or 1:64 per OLT port
  • Total bandwidth: 2.5 Gb/s downstream, 1.25 Gb/s upstream (shared among subscribers)

ODF (Optical Distribution Frame)

Optical patching cabinet located at the POP and distribution points. The ODF connects fibers from customers to OLT ports via pigtails and SC/APC or SC/UPC adapters.

How It Works — Light Transmission

Fiber optics exploits the principle of total internal reflection. The glass core (silica) has a higher refractive index than the optical cladding. When light is injected into the core at an angle less than the critical angle, it remains trapped inside and propagates through successive reflections.

Wavelengths used:

  • 1310 nm: upstream (client → POP), attenuation ~0.35 dB/km
  • 1490 nm: downstream (POP → client), attenuation ~0.25 dB/km
  • 1550 nm: used for long distance links, attenuation ~0.20 dB/km

Complete FTTH Installation Process (OLT → Client)

Step 1: Planning

  1. Identify the neighborhood to be fiberized (e.g., Nlongkak, Bastos, Mvan in Yaoundé)
  2. Map existing roads and underground infrastructure
  3. Size the number of potential customers and required OLT ports
  4. Choose locations for street ODF cabinets and splitters

Step 2: Backbone Deployment

  1. Run fiber from the POP to distribution points (street cabinets)
  2. Use cables with 12 to 144 fibers (single mode G.652D)
  3. Splice cables together using fusion splicing
  4. Test each fiber with an OTDR to verify losses

Step 3: Distribution Network Deployment

  1. Install splitters (1:8 or 1:16) in ODF cabinets
  2. Run drop cables from cabinets to homes
  3. These cables typically contain 1 to 4 fibers

Step 4: Customer Installation

  1. Position the fiber entry point in the premises
  2. Splice an SC/APC pigtail onto the drop cable
  3. Test continuity with a VFL (Visual Fault Locator)
  4. Connect the fiber to the ONT
  5. Power the ONT and verify synchronization with the OLT
  6. Configure the customer's WiFi router
  7. Run a speed test and validate the installation

Step 5: Service Activation

  1. The ONT registers with the OLT (authentication by serial number or LOID)
  2. The OLT assigns dynamic bandwidth (DBA - Dynamic Bandwidth Allocation)
  3. Traffic is encapsulated in GPON frames (GEM frames)
  4. Data is transported to the POP, then routed to the Internet backbone

Optical Power Budget Calculation

The power budget is the difference between the OLT transmit power and the minimum ONT receiver sensitivity. It must be sufficient to compensate for all losses.

Calculation Example

Element Loss (dB) Quantity Subtotal (dB)
OLT transmit power +5 dBm 1 +5.0
ONT sensitivity -27 dBm 1 -27.0
Total available budget 32.0 dB
Fiber (0.35 dB/km × 5 km) -1.75 1 -1.75
Splitter 1:8 -10.5 1 -10.5
Splitter 1:4 -7.2 1 -7.2
SC/APC connectors (0.25 dB/conn.) -0.25 6 -1.5
Fusion splices (0.05 dB/splice) -0.05 8 -0.4
Total estimated losses -21.35 dB
Remaining margin 10.65 dB

A minimum margin of 3 dB is recommended. Here we have 10.65 dB, which is excellent. If the margin were negative, you would need to: reduce the distance, use a splitter with a lower ratio, or choose an OLT with higher transmit power.

Tools Used

OTDR (Optical Time Domain Reflectometer)
Usage:
  • Connect the OTDR to the end of the fiber to be tested
  • Configure the wavelength (1310 nm, 1550 nm)
  • Start acquisition to obtain the backscatter trace
  • Analyze the trace (fiber path, connector losses, splice losses, reflective events)
  • Check total distance and cumulative optical loss
  • Save the SOR file for the installation report
  • Common models: EXFO FTB-200, Yokogawa AQ1200, Fluke OFP-100
# Typical OTDR parameters for FTTH:
Wavelength: 1310 nm (or 1550 nm for long distance)
Pulse duration: 10-100 ns (depending on distance)
Range: 10-20 km
Refractive index: 1.4682 (G.652D)
Fault threshold: event > 0.1 dB
Fiber end threshold: 1.5 dB
Power Meter & Laser Source
Usage:
  • Connect the laser source to end A of the fiber
  • Connect the power meter to end B
  • Measure received power in dBm
  • Subtract transmitted power to obtain total attenuation
  • Compare with the calculated power budget
  • Use clean adapters and references to avoid errors
# Measurement example:
Transmit power (source): -5 dBm at 1310 nm
Received power (power meter): -18.7 dBm
Total loss: -18.7 - (-5.0) = -13.7 dB
Result: within acceptable margin (< 20 dB)
Fusion Splicer
Usage:
  • Strip the fibers with a stripper (precision 250 µm, 900 µm)
  • Clean the fibers with isopropyl alcohol and a lint-free wipe
  • Cleave the fibers with a precision cleaver (cut angle < 0.5°)
  • Place the fibers in the fusion splicer
  • Start the automatic cycle: alignment, pre-fusion, fusion, tension test
  • Check estimated loss (< 0.05 dB)
  • Protect the splice with a heat-shrink sleeve
  • Models: Fujikura 70S+, Sumitomo T-600C, Signal Fire AI-9
VFL (Visual Fault Locator)
Usage:
  • Connect the VFL (visible red laser) to the fiber to be tested
  • The red beam travels through the fiber and is visible at break points
  • If the fiber is broken, red light is visible at the break location
  • Useful for identifying a specific fiber among many in an ODF (continuity check)
  • Do not look directly at the laser output!

Practical Case at Matrix Telecom Yaoundé

FTTH Deployment in the Mvan Neighborhood

Matrix Telecom fiberized the Mvan neighborhood in Yaoundé (dense residential area). Here is the actual deployment:

  • Matrix Mvan POP: 1 rack OLT Huawei MA5800-X15 with 4 GPON cards (64 ports each)
  • Backbone: 48-fiber G.652D cable run from POP to 4 street cabinets (AME-01 to AME-04)
  • Distribution: Each cabinet contains 2 splitters 1:8 + 1 splitter 1:4 at the input
  • Connection: Drop cables of 100-300m from cabinets to villas
  • ONT: Huawei HG8245Q (triple play: Internet + TV + VoIP)
  • OTDR tests: Average total loss of 18 dB for the farthest customers (3.5 km from POP)
  • Result: 256 customers connected with a guaranteed speed of 100 Mb/s symmetric

Tip for the Intern

Tip:
  • Always clean optical connectors before using them (with a cleaning kit or isopropyl alcohol). A dirty connector is the #1 cause of optical loss.
  • NEVER look directly at the end of a fiber powered by a laser. The laser can damage your retina.
  • Use a protective cap on every unused connector to prevent dust.
  • During cleaving, the cut surface must be perfectly perpendicular (< 0.5°). Poor cleaving results in high loss.
  • Record all OTDR measurements and fiber references in a log. Traceability is crucial for maintenance.
  • In the power budget, always plan a safety margin of 3 dB for future connections and component aging.
  • Learn to read an OTDR trace: a reflective event (peak) indicates a connector or break. A non-reflective event (step) indicates a splice or micro-bend.
  • For FTTH deployment, prefer splitters 1:8 to 1:16 to avoid excessive loss.
  • The fiber cable must be handled with care: minimum bend radius of 30 mm (10 times the cable diameter).

Exercise

Task 1: Splice and Test a Fiber Optic Pigtail with an OTDR

Fiber splicing is a hands-on skill used daily by Matrix field technicians when deploying FTTH or repairing backbone links. This task simulates the fusion splicing workflow and OTDR testing that you would perform in the field or lab.

  1. Strip the fiber cable jacket 1 meter from the end using a fiber stripping tool (0.25 mm for tight buffer).
  2. Clean the bare fiber with isopropyl alcohol and a lint-free wipe.
  3. Cleave the fiber with a precision cleaver — the cleave angle must be < 0.5° for low-loss splicing.
  4. Place both fibers into the fusion splicer (e.g., Fujikura 70S) and press Arc to splice.
  5. Check the estimated loss displayed on the splicer: good splice < 0.05 dB, acceptable < 0.1 dB. Re-splice if higher.
  6. Connect the spliced fiber to an OTDR (EXFO FTB-200) with a launch cable: set range=5 km, pulse=10 ns, wavelength=1310 nm.
  7. Analyze the trace: identify the launch pulse, the splice event (small step loss), and the far-end reflection.
  8. Record the splice loss and distance in the installation log.

Task 2: Perform a Power Budget Calculation for an FTTH Link

Before deploying FTTH, Matrix engineers calculate the optical power budget to ensure the received signal is within the ONT's sensitivity range. This task walks through a realistic FTTH power budget calculation.

  1. Gather parameters: OLT transmit power = +2 dBm, 1:16 splitter loss = 13.8 dB, fiber distance = 3 km, fiber attenuation = 0.35 dB/km (1310 nm).
  2. Calculate total fiber loss: 3 km × 0.35 dB/km = 1.05 dB.
  3. Add splice and connector losses: 2 splices × 0.1 dB each + 4 connectors × 0.3 dB each = 1.4 dB.
  4. Total link loss: splitter loss + fiber loss + splice/connector loss = 13.8 + 1.05 + 1.4 = 16.25 dB.
  5. Received power at ONT: OLT Tx - total loss = +2 - 16.25 = -14.25 dBm.
  6. Check ONT sensitivity (typically -27 dBm): -14.25 dBm > -27 dBm, so the link budget is acceptable.
  7. Add a 3 dB safety margin: received power should be at least -24 dBm after margin. Our -14.25 dBm passes.
  8. Document the budget in the project file and confirm with an OTDR measurement on the installed link.
